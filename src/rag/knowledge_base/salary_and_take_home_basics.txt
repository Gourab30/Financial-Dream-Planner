Salary Structure Basics for Freshers (Educational only - not tax advice)

CTC vs in-hand salary: Cost to Company (CTC) is the total yearly cost an
employer attributes to an employee, and it is usually higher than the
amount that actually reaches the employee's bank account each month
("in-hand" or take-home salary). CTC commonly bundles together the basic
salary, allowances, employer contributions to retirement/provident
schemes, and sometimes the value of other benefits, so CTC divided by 12
overstates the real monthly cash available to invest.

Why monthly salary is predicted, not entered directly: This project
predicts a fresher's likely current monthly salary from Age, City,
Education and Job Role using a trained regression model, rather than
asking the user to type in a salary figure, so that a user who does not
yet have an offer can still get a realistic planning estimate based on
patterns in similar freshers' salaries.

Typical salary deductions: A salaried employee's monthly payslip commonly
shows deductions such as employee provident fund (EPF) contributions,
professional tax (where applicable in that state), and income tax
deducted at source (TDS) where applicable, in addition to the take-home
amount actually paid out. The exact amounts depend on the employer's
salary structure and prevailing tax rules, which this project does not
calculate.

Available monthly capacity in this project: This project's "available
monthly capacity" is the user's chosen savings percentage applied to the
predicted monthly salary. It represents money the user has said they want
to direct toward these goals, not a prediction of how much they will have
left after every possible expense, so real-world essential expenses
(rent, food, transport, existing EMIs) should already be accounted for
before deciding on that savings percentage.

Salary growth over time: This project predicts a snapshot monthly salary
for the age and profile supplied; it does not project future salary
increments or promotions over the goal horizon. Because of this, the
required monthly SIP figures are conservative in the sense that rising
income over a long career is not factored in to reduce the burden in
later years.
