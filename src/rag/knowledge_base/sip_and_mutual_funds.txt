SIP and Mutual Fund Basics (Educational only - no specific fund is recommended)

What is a SIP: A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way of investing a
fixed amount into a mutual fund at a regular interval (usually monthly)
instead of investing the whole amount at once. This project's required
monthly SIP for each goal is exactly this kind of regular monthly
investment, calculated so that regular contributions plus assumed growth
reach the target amount by the goal year.

Lump sum vs SIP: A lump sum is a one-time investment of the full amount,
while a SIP spreads the investment across many months. SIPs are often
preferred by salaried earners because they match a monthly income and
apply rupee-cost averaging - buying more mutual fund units when prices are
low and fewer when prices are high, which smooths out the impact of market
ups and downs compared to investing everything on a single date.

NAV and units: A mutual fund pools money from many investors and buys a
basket of underlying investments (stocks, bonds, or both). Each investor
owns "units" of the fund, and the Net Asset Value (NAV) is the price of
one unit on a given day. A SIP instalment buys however many units that
month's contribution amount can afford at that day's NAV.

Expense ratio: This is the annual fee a mutual fund charges, expressed as
a percentage of the invested amount, to cover fund management and
operating costs. A lower expense ratio means more of the investment
return stays with the investor rather than going toward fees.

Active vs index (passive) funds: An actively managed fund has a fund
manager choosing investments to try to beat a market benchmark, usually
at a higher expense ratio. An index fund simply tracks a market benchmark
(such as a broad equity index) and typically has a lower expense ratio.
Neither type is guaranteed to deliver a specific return.

ELSS and lock-in: An Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) is a category of
equity mutual fund that comes with a mandatory lock-in period (commonly
three years) during which units cannot be withdrawn. Some mutual fund
categories, including ELSS, may also be associated with tax benefits under
Indian tax law, but this project does not calculate or advise on tax
savings.

Step-up SIP: A step-up (or top-up) SIP is one where the monthly
instalment is increased periodically, for example once a year in line
with a salary increment, instead of staying fixed for the whole goal
horizon. This project's own SIP figures assume a flat monthly amount
throughout, so a step-up approach would generally need a smaller starting
instalment than the flat figure shown here to reach the same goal.

CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is a single smoothed-out annual
growth rate that describes how an investment grew from a starting value
to an ending value over a period, ignoring the ups and downs along the
way. It is a common way to compare the historical performance of
different investments over the same time period.
