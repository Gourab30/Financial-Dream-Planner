General Financial Planning Guidelines (Educational, not professional advice)

Emergency fund: Before investing towards long-term goals, it is generally
recommended to build an emergency fund covering 3 to 6 months of essential
monthly expenses, kept in a liquid, low-risk instrument such as a savings
account or a liquid mutual fund.

Start early: Because of compounding, starting a Systematic Investment Plan
(SIP) a few years earlier can substantially reduce the monthly amount needed
to reach the same goal. Time in the market generally matters more than
timing the market.

Automate investments: Setting up an automatic monthly transfer into an
investment account (an SIP) removes the need to remember to invest and
reduces the temptation to skip a month.

Review periodically: A financial plan should be reviewed at least once a
year, or whenever there is a major life change (job change, marriage,
relocation), because salary, expenses and goal costs change over time.

Diversification: Spreading investments across multiple asset classes
(equity, debt, gold, etc.) instead of a single instrument can help reduce
the impact of any one investment performing poorly.

Debt before investing: High-interest debt (such as credit card debt)
should generally be paid down before starting new discretionary
investments, because the interest cost usually exceeds typical investment
returns.

Inflation matters: The cost of large goals such as a home, a car or a
wedding tends to rise every year because of inflation. Planning using
today's price alone, without adjusting for inflation, will usually
under-estimate how much is required in the future.
