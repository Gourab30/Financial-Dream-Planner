Financial Planning & Goal Investment Notes (educational only, not professional advice)

GENERAL GUIDELINES

Emergency fund: Before investing towards long-term goals, it is generally
recommended to build an emergency fund covering 3 to 6 months of essential
monthly expenses, kept in a liquid, low-risk instrument such as a savings
account or a liquid mutual fund.

Start early: Because of compounding, starting a Systematic Investment Plan
(SIP) a few years earlier can substantially reduce the monthly amount needed
to reach the same goal. Time in the market generally matters more than
timing the market.

Automate investments: Setting up an automatic monthly transfer into an
investment account (an SIP) removes the need to remember to invest and
reduces the temptation to skip a month.

Review periodically: A financial plan should be reviewed at least once a
year, or whenever there is a major life change (job change, marriage,
relocation), because salary, expenses and goal costs change over time.

Diversification: Spreading investments across multiple asset classes
(equity, debt, gold, etc.) instead of a single instrument can help reduce
the impact of any one investment performing poorly.

Debt before investing: High-interest debt (such as credit card debt)
should generally be paid down before starting new discretionary
investments, because the interest cost usually exceeds typical investment
returns.

Inflation matters: The cost of large goals such as a home, a car or a
wedding tends to rise every year because of inflation. Planning using
today's price alone, without adjusting for inflation, will usually
under-estimate how much is required in the future.

HOW THIS PROJECT CALCULATES A PLAN

Inflation assumption: Future goal costs are projected forward from today's
city-level base cost using a fixed annual inflation rate applied
compound-yearly: future_cost = base_cost * (1 + inflation_rate) ^ years.

Required monthly investment: The monthly SIP needed to reach a future goal
amount is calculated using the standard future-value-of-an-annuity-due
formula, assuming the expected annual investment return is compounded
monthly and contributions are made at the start of each month.

Feasibility classification: This project compares the required monthly
SIP for a goal against the user's available monthly investment capacity
(their chosen savings percentage multiplied by their predicted monthly
salary):
  - Achievable: required amount is fully covered by available capacity.
  - Challenging: required amount exceeds capacity by up to 25%.
  - Highly Challenging: required amount exceeds capacity by more than 25%.

Ways to close a shortfall: If a goal is Challenging or Highly Challenging,
the user can reduce the gap by increasing the goal timeline (more years to
save), increasing their savings percentage, choosing a lower-cost city or
goal amount, or a combination of these.

This project is built around freshers / early-career professionals, so
years of work experience is never used as an input to the salary
prediction model.

Goal-wise planning: Marriage, New Car and New Home are always planned as
separate goals with independent timelines, because a user's target date
for each goal is usually different.

INVESTMENT CATEGORIES

Short-term category (goal is 3 years away or less): a capital-preservation
oriented category, such as debt-oriented mutual funds, fixed deposits or
liquid funds. These typically prioritise protecting the invested amount
over maximising growth, because there is little time to recover from a
market downturn before the goal is due.

Medium-term category (goal is 4 to 7 years away): a diversified balanced
category, such as hybrid mutual funds that mix equity and debt. This aims
to balance growth potential with reduced volatility compared to a pure
equity portfolio.

Long-term category (goal is more than 7 years away): a diversified
long-term growth oriented category, such as diversified equity mutual
funds. Longer time horizons generally allow more time to ride out
short-term market volatility in pursuit of higher long-term growth.

Limits: this project never recommends an individual stock, fund house or
ticker, and never guarantees a rate of return - expected_investment_return
is a user-adjustable assumption, not a promise. These categories are broad
educational buckets only, meant to illustrate matching investment risk to
time horizon.
