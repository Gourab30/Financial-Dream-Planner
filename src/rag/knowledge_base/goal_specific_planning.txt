Planning For Marriage, New Car and New Home (Educational only)

Marriage/wedding budgeting: A wedding budget in India commonly includes
venue and catering, jewellery and outfits, photography and videography,
decor, and guest hospitality, and costs vary widely by city, guest count
and scale. This project's Marriage goal uses a single city-level average
cost as a starting estimate; an actual wedding budget should be built
bottom-up from these individual components for a more precise figure.

New car planning: Buying a car usually involves either paying the full
on-road price upfront or taking a car loan and paying a down payment
(commonly 10-20% of the price) with the rest financed over a loan tenure.
This project's New Car goal assumes the full future cost is accumulated
through SIP savings by the goal year, which is one approach; a car loan
combined with a smaller SIP is another common alternative not modelled
here.

New home planning: A home purchase typically requires a down payment
(commonly around 20% of the property value, since most home loans cover
up to about 75-80% loan-to-value) plus additional costs such as stamp
duty, registration charges and interior/furnishing costs, which are on
top of the base property price. This project's New Home goal projects
only the base property cost forward with inflation; a real home-buying
budget should add these additional costs separately.

Why goals are planned separately: Marriage, New Car and New Home are
modelled as independent goals with independent timelines rather than one
combined number, because a person's target year for each goal is usually
different, and because seeing each goal's own required SIP and status
makes it clear which specific goal is driving a shortfall, rather than
hiding that detail inside a single combined figure.

Choosing which goals to plan for: A user does not need to select all
three goals at once. Selecting only the goals that are actually relevant
in the near term, and adding others later as they become relevant, is a
reasonable way to keep the required monthly SIP realistic relative to
current income.

Prioritising when there is a shortfall: When the combined required SIP
across all selected goals exceeds available monthly capacity, common ways
to prioritise include ranking goals by which has the nearest target year,
which is most essential, or which has the smallest funding gap and is
therefore easiest to make Achievable first.
