Quick Financial Terms Glossary (Educational, short definitions only)

SIP: Systematic Investment Plan - investing a fixed amount at regular
intervals (usually monthly) rather than all at once.

NAV: Net Asset Value - the price of a single unit of a mutual fund on a
given day.

CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Rate - a single smoothed annual growth rate
describing how an investment grew from a start value to an end value over
a period.

Equity: Ownership shares in a company; equity investments (such as
company stocks or equity mutual funds) generally carry higher potential
growth and higher short-term volatility than debt investments.

Debt fund: A mutual fund category that invests mainly in fixed-income
instruments such as bonds and government securities, generally aiming for
more stable, lower-volatility returns than equity.

Hybrid fund: A mutual fund category that mixes both equity and debt in a
single fund, aiming to balance growth potential with reduced volatility
compared to a pure equity fund.

Liquid fund: A category of debt mutual fund that invests in very
short-term instruments, used mainly for parking money that may be needed
on short notice, such as an emergency fund, rather than for long-term
growth.

ELSS: Equity Linked Savings Scheme - a category of equity mutual fund
with a mandatory lock-in period, sometimes associated with tax benefits
under Indian tax law.

Asset allocation: How an investor's total money is divided across
different asset classes (such as equity, debt and gold), based on goals,
time horizon and risk appetite.

Risk appetite: How much variability or potential short-term loss in
investment value an investor is comfortable accepting in pursuit of
higher long-term returns.

Liquidity: How quickly and easily an investment can be converted back
into cash without a significant loss of value; a savings account is
highly liquid, while property is not.

Compounding: Earning returns not only on the original invested amount but
also on the returns that amount has already earned, so growth accelerates
the longer money stays invested.
